## How to Read GFCRI

GFCRI stands for Global Financial Crisis Risk Index.

It is a daily macro-risk monitor designed to answer five questions:

1. Is systemic macro-financial pressure rising?
2. What is driving the pressure?
3. Is risk spreading through transmission channels?
4. Is hidden risk building under the surface?
5. What should users watch next?

GFCRI is not a trading signal and not a crisis prediction engine.

It is a structured way to read macro risk across rates, FX, credit, equities, commodities, sentiment, trade spillover, and hidden risk.

## Start With the Headline Score

The GFCRI score ranges from 0 to 100.

A simple interpretation:

| GFCRI range | Reading | Meaning |
|---:|---|---|
| 0-25 | Normal / contained | Systemic pressure is relatively low |
| 25-45 | Elevated | Some pressure is building |
| 45-60 | Warning | Multiple risk areas may be active |
| 60+ | Critical stress | Broad systemic pressure may be present |

These ranges are not investment instructions.

They are risk-monitoring bands. The score tells you where to look next, not what to buy or sell.

## Read the Alert Level

GFCRI uses alert language to make the score easier to interpret:

- Normal;
- Elevated;
- Warning;
- Critical Stress.

The alert level is useful for fast scanning.

But the real value comes from reading the drivers behind the alert.

A score without drivers is just a number. GFCRI is designed to show why the number changed.

## Check the Index Date and Data Status

Before interpreting any risk score, check the date and data status.

A current reading should tell you:

- the latest observation date;
- whether market data is fresh;
- whether any critical data is missing or stale;
- whether the official index update was held because of data quality.

GFCRI includes data freshness controls because stale or missing critical data can make a risk reading misleading.

If the system says an index is held from the last reliable observation, do not interpret that as risk stability. Interpret it as a data-quality control.

## Read the Top Drivers

The next step is to identify what is driving the score.

Top drivers can come from:

- rates;
- FX;
- credit;
- equities;
- commodities;
- sentiment;
- hidden-risk signals;
- trade spillover.

A useful question:

```text
Is this one isolated driver, or are several domains confirming the same pressure?
```

If only one indicator is elevated, the signal may be narrow. If rates, FX, credit, and commodities all confirm pressure, the signal is more important.

## Understand Directional Pressure

Not every large move is a risk signal.

For example:

- an equity index rising can be statistically unusual but not necessarily stressful;
- a credit spread widening is more directly stressful;
- dollar strength can tighten global financial conditions;
- a commodity spike can pressure inflation and margins;
- falling yields can signal growth fear in some regimes but relief in others.

GFCRI separates statistical anomaly from risk-directional pressure.

This is important because a market can move a lot in a good direction.

The model should not treat every unusual move as stress.

## Read the 1Y Trend

The 1Y GFCRI trend shows whether macro risk is rising, falling, or moving sideways over time.

Do not overreact to one day.

Instead, ask:

```text
Is the risk score deteriorating for several observations?
```

Useful trend questions:

- Is the current score higher than last month?
- Is the score rising while volatility stays low?
- Are risk spikes becoming more frequent?
- Did a recent improvement fully reverse, or only pause?

The 1Y trend is useful because macro risk often builds gradually before it becomes obvious.

## Read Hidden Risk

Hidden risk is one of the most important GFCRI sections.

It captures cases where surface stress looks calm but underlying pressure remains elevated.

Examples:

- low VIX but high rates;
- calm equities but stressed FX;
- strong index performance but crowded concentration;
- stable credit ETFs but deteriorating credit proxies;
- elevated gold while headline volatility is subdued.

GFCRI's hidden-risk reading helps users avoid treating calm markets as automatically safe markets.

Hidden risk is not a crisis call. It is a watch signal.

## Read Transmission Channels

Transmission channels show how risk can move through the system.

Examples:

```text
US rates -> DXY -> KRW/USD -> KOSPI
```

```text
Credit stress -> high yield -> sovereign risk -> equities
```

```text
China stress -> Hang Seng -> Korea exports -> Asian equities
```

A channel becomes more important when multiple nodes inside the path show pressure together.

Useful questions:

- Is the channel active?
- Which node is leading?
- Is the pressure spreading or contained?
- Is the transmission financial, macro, or sentiment-driven?

## Read the Evidence Summary

GFCRI's evidence view exists for users who want to go deeper.

It includes:

- sub-index breakdown;
- node contribution table;
- transmission-chain details;
- hidden-risk evidence;
- formula receipt;
- source-tier and methodology context.

Most users should start with the brief.

Use evidence when you need to verify why the score moved.

## Read "Watch Next"

The Watch Next section is designed to turn the risk reading into a monitoring plan.

Examples:

- watch whether dollar strength pressures emerging-market FX;
- watch whether credit spreads widen;
- watch whether hidden pressure converts into visible volatility;
- watch whether equity concentration reverses;
- watch whether commodity stress spreads into inflation-sensitive assets.

This is not a trading instruction.

It is a risk-monitoring checklist.

## A Practical Daily Workflow

Here is a simple way to use GFCRI:

### 1. Read the score

```text
What is the GFCRI level today?
```

### 2. Read the trend

```text
Is the score rising or falling over the past year?
```

### 3. Read the drivers

```text
Which domains are contributing most?
```

### 4. Read hidden risk

```text
Is the market calmer on the surface than underneath?
```

### 5. Read transmission

```text
Are risk channels active or dormant?
```

### 6. Read watch next

```text
What should I monitor over the next few sessions?
```

## Common Misreadings

### Misreading 1: "A yellow score means sell."

No.

A yellow or elevated score means pressure is worth monitoring. It does not mean sell or hedge by default.

### Misreading 2: "Low VIX means GFCRI should be low."

Not necessarily.

GFCRI includes more than surface volatility. Rates, FX, credit, commodities, and hidden-risk signals can matter even when VIX is low.

### Misreading 3: "A high score predicts a crisis."

No.

GFCRI monitors risk pressure. It does not forecast exact crisis timing.

### Misreading 4: "One driver explains everything."

Usually not.

Macro risk is often a combination of drivers and transmission channels.

## What GFCRI Is Best For

GFCRI is best used as:

- a daily macro-risk brief;
- a cross-asset pressure monitor;
- a hidden-risk scanner;
- a watchlist generator;
- a framework for understanding risk transmission.

It is not meant to replace professional judgment, portfolio policy, or personalized advice.
